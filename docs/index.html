Wayfinder · Agentic Ontology

Your agent.Your rules.

Wayfinder is a personal AI agent that acts for you from facts you own and rules you set, and records every step in a ledger you keep. A small ontology decides what it may believe, reveal and do; the ledger shows what it did and why.

01 · The problem

Agents now run errands. Nobody can check them.

Personal AI agents are moving from answering questions to booking, buying and negotiating with other agents. As far as we can tell from how mainstream assistants work today:

01

Knowledge is loose text

Chat history and notes cannot tell a checked fact from a guess, or notice when a fact has gone stale.

02

Rules are all-or-nothing

A tool is on or off. You cannot say "never share my bank statements" or "never agree for me".

03

No record of why

After a negotiation: what did it give up, how close did it come to my limits, and why did it accept?

04

The vendor owns the memory

You cannot easily inspect, correct, export or move it.

Evidence that others have this problem has not been gathered yet. Five interviews are planned before 2027-01-08.

02 · The idea

One small ontology. Four jobs.

An ontology here is a short, written-down list of the kinds of things in a person's world (an event, a lease, a passport) and how they may relate. It acts as a contract for the agent. Each job builds on TrustGraph, used unmodified.

What it may believe

Facts from your documents must fit the model. Critical facts such as dates and amounts are used only after you confirm them.

OntologyRAG · domain, range, subclass checks

What it may reveal

Consent rules are written against types: "financial records: never"; "income letter: may be shared for a lease".

Gate · SHACL-AF policies as a second layer

What it may do

The agent chooses from a fixed set of options. It can draft and propose, but cannot pay or agree. Only you mark a clause agreed.

Constrained decisions · signed mandates

Why it did it

Every answer links to the facts it used and the source of each one, through the personal ledger.

PROV-O lineage · personal ledger
03 · The personal ledger

Written first. Kept by you.

Every step the agent takes is written, in order, to a ledger you keep. A single gate writes each entry before the action happens. If the write fails, nothing happens.

Provenance

Where did this come from?

Each fact and claim, with its source and a content hash.

Explicability

Why did it do that?

The options it had, the rule that narrowed them, and the signal that picked one.

Observability

What is it doing now?

Status, timings, models used, errors, denials, and approvals waiting for you.

What the integrity claim covers

Claimed: against a sealed run's head commitment, kept separately, the ledger shows whether any entry, or any evidence it hashes, was later changed, inserted, deleted, reordered, cut off the end, or rolled back.

Not claimed:

  • that an entry is true;
  • that nothing was left out before it was written;
  • that timestamps are exact.
04 · How it runs

Local, containerised, on your hardware.

Everything runs on one machine, in containers, with local models and no hosted services. The same commands run on five platform profiles; only configuration differs. Each outside connection sits behind an adapter, so hosted models or network agents can be added later.

05 · Four scenarios

Tested on synthetic lives. Pass rules fixed first.

One made-up persona, made-up counterparties and saved web pages. Every run writes a ledger; every pass rule is an acceptance ID in the concept (A-01 to A-18).

S1Must

Get me to the conference

Typed, confirmed facts → purpose-bound sharing → a booking drafted but not paid → "why this hotel?"

S2Should

The world changed

Two dated snapshots disagree on the venue, and a passport-validity rule breaks the plan. The agent must stop and ask.

S3Must

Lease renewal, agent to agent

Six issues negotiated inside a signed mandate. Only the person can agree. Bank statements: never.

S4Must

The hostile counterparty

Ten attacks, three hidden inside valid fields. No blocked disclosure; each attempt is recorded.
