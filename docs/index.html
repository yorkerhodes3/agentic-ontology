Wayfinder

A personal AI agent that acts for you from facts you own and rules you set, and keeps a step-by-step record of everything it did, so you can trust it, check it, and keep your personal information in your own hands.

The problem

Personal AI agents are moving from answering questions to running errands: booking, buying, and now negotiating with other agents. Today (as far as we can tell from how mainstream assistants work):

Knowledge is loose text

Chat history and notes cannot tell a checked fact from a guess, or notice when a fact has gone stale.

Rules are all-or-nothing

A tool is on or off. You cannot say "never share my bank statements" or "never agree for me".

No record of why

After a negotiation: what did it give up, how close did it come to my limits, and why did it accept?

The vendor owns the memory

You cannot easily inspect, correct, export or move it.

Evidence that others have this problem has not been gathered yet. Five interviews are planned before 2027-01-08.

One small ontology, four jobs

An ontology here is a short, written-down list of the kinds of things in a person's world (an event, a lease, a passport) and how they may relate. It acts as a contract for the agent.

Job 1

What it may believe

Facts pulled from your documents must fit the model. Critical facts such as dates and amounts are used only after you confirm them.

Job 2

What it may reveal

Consent rules are written against types: "financial records: never"; "income letter: may be shared for a lease".

Job 3

What it may do

The agent chooses from a fixed set of options. It can draft and propose, but cannot pay or agree.

Job 4

Why it did it

Every answer links to the facts it used and the source of each one.

Builds on TrustGraph (Apache-2.0): ontology-guided extraction, SHACL-AF policies, PROV-O provenance and local decision models. TrustGraph is used unmodified.

The personal ledger

Every step the agent takes is written, in order, to a ledger you keep. A single gate writes each entry before the action happens. If the write fails, nothing happens.

Provenance

Where did this come from? Each fact and claim, with its source and a content hash.

Explicability

Why did it do that? The options it had, the rule that narrowed them, and the signal that picked one.

Observability

What is it doing now? Status, timings, models used, errors, denials, and approvals waiting for you.

What the integrity claim covers

Claimed: against a sealed run's head commitment, kept separately, the ledger shows whether any entry, or any evidence it hashes, was later edited, inserted, deleted, reordered, cut off the end, or rolled back.

Not claimed: that an entry is true; that nothing was left out before it was written; that timestamps are exact.

How it runs in v0.4: local and containerised

Everything runs on one machine, in containers, with local models. No hosted services. Each outside connection sits behind an adapter, so hosted models, network agents or public anchoring can be added later.

Four scenarios to test the idea

All synthetic: one made-up persona, made-up counterparties, saved web pages. Pass rules are fixed before anything runs.

S1 · Must

Get me to the conference

Typed, confirmed facts → purpose-bound sharing → a booking drafted but not paid → "why this hotel?"

S2 · Should

The world changed

Two dated snapshots disagree on the venue, and a passport-validity rule breaks the plan. The agent must stop and ask.

S3 · Must

Lease renewal, agent-to-agent

Six issues negotiated inside a signed mandate. Only the person can agree. Bank statements: never.

S4 · Must

The hostile counterparty

Ten attacks, three hidden inside valid fields. No blocked disclosure; each attempt is recorded.

"What did my agent negotiate?"

"Inside the mandate" is not the whole story. Below is a real run of the Neocloud Supply OS negotiation room, which S3 reuses. It is a GPU-rack purchase, not the lease: seed 42, counterpart Boulware, ours linear, 6 rounds, engine commit f973c50.

Across 20 seeded trials with the same styles: 7 settled, 13 escalated to a human, 0 walked away. In S3, Wayfinder turns this kind of evidence into a clause-by-clause readout. Every concession links to the ledger entry that explains it, and the landlord's copy never shows your limits.
